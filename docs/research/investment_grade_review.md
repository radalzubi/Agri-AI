# Investment-Grade Review

Updated: 2026-03-23

## Executive Take

The original idea was directionally right about the problem and wrong about the entry point.

The real pain is not "farms need better planning software."
The real pain is that buyer demand, grower commitments, and seasonal supply risk are coordinated through spreadsheets, calls, and institutional memory.

That makes the strongest initial customer:

- buyer-facing aggregator
- food hub
- regional produce operator
- local sourcing coordinator tied to recurring buyers

Not the strongest initial customer:

- individual farm

## What Held Up Under Review

- moving the customer up-chain from farmer to aggregator is correct
- early AI claims are overstated and should be avoided
- monthly SaaS alone is weak for a seasonal planning problem
- data and workflow ownership matter more than a recommendation engine
- the business has to start with a narrow wedge tied to visible operator pain

## What Did Not Hold Up

- the claim that the category is uncontested is false
- the claim that food hubs will reliably pay enterprise software pricing is weak
- the demand exchange idea is not a Year 1 motion
- the financing layer is structurally too early and too capital-intensive

## Bottom-Line Conclusion

This is worth pursuing only as a narrow operator workflow business:

turn buyer demand into grower commitments before planting, then track fill-risk weekly once the season begins.

The first product should be sold as planning infrastructure for aggregators serving institutional or recurring wholesale buyers.

## Best Near-Term Positioning

Use this framing:

Agri-AI helps aggregators and regional produce operators decide what their grower network should commit before planting, so they can fill more buyer demand and reduce emergency buys.

Avoid this framing:

- AI-native farm planning platform
- operating system for all agriculture
- marketplace or fintech company from day one

## Immediate Validation Questions

1. Will an aggregator or local sourcing operator pay for pre-season planning help before a full platform exists?
2. Can the product show value inside one season?
3. Is the data available in a format usable enough to support weekly planning?
4. Does the operator care enough about shortage and overage risk to change workflow?
