# Market Reality

## Core Market Truth

This is not a broad agriculture software opportunity in its first phase.
It is a specialty-crop supply coordination opportunity.

## Current State

Growers and operators already plan, but they do so in fragmented ways.

Planning is spread across:

- spreadsheets
- email
- phone calls
- buyer memory
- grower assumptions

The problem is not that nobody plans.
The problem is that nobody coordinates the full picture well before the season starts.

## Why Adoption Is Hard

- farms are not ideal first customers
- many operators have limited software budgets
- outcome cycles are seasonal, not monthly
- data is inconsistent across buyers and growers
- trust matters more than dashboards

## Indiana / Midwest Implication

Indiana is a strong pilot geography but not proof of a universal category.

The initial market must stay focused on operators serving recurring specialty-crop demand, especially where buyer reliability matters.

## Commercial Implication

The best first sale is a paid pilot tied to visible operator pain, not a broad platform contract.
