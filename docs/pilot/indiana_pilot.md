# Indiana Pilot

## Pilot Objective

Prove that Agri-AI can improve pre-season and in-season planning for a buyer-facing agricultural operator in Indiana by making supply risk visible before inventory exists.

## Ideal First Customers

Target one of these profiles:

- Indiana food hub serving schools, hospitals, or institutional buyers
- regional produce distributor with a local sourcing program
- multi-grower aggregator managing recurring buyer demand

Selection criteria:

- at least 10 active growers or suppliers
- recurring seasonal buyer demand
- historical order data available in any format
- visible issues with shortages, substitutions, emergency buys, or overproduction
- one decision-maker willing to meet weekly

## Data To Collect

Minimum data:

- 12 to 24 months of order history by crop, customer, quantity, and week
- unfilled orders or substitution history
- grower roster with crop timing and expected capacity
- crop calendar or planting assumptions
- buyer requirements such as pack size, grade, and delivery window

Useful optional data:

- emergency purchase history
- margin data
- spoilage or rejection data
- route and delivery constraints

## Weekly Output

Each week the pilot should produce:

- demand-by-crop and week view
- expected supply coverage view
- shortage and overage risk report
- operator recommendations for grower or buyer action
- open issues requiring follow-up

Pre-season output should also include:

- baseline demand map
- top demand concentration risks
- first-pass grower commitment plan

## Success Metrics

Pilot success should be measured through at least two of the following:

- improvement in planned demand coverage
- reduction in emergency buys
- reduction in unfilled lines or substitutions
- reduction in overproduction or unsold volume
- operator time saved in weekly planning

## Outreach Angle

Pitch the pilot as a decision-support workflow, not a heavy software rollout.

Use this angle:

We help you turn buyer demand into grower commitments before planting and flag shortage risk early enough to act.

Avoid positioning it as:

- a generic AI platform
- a replacement for all existing systems
- a long integration project

## Indiana Target Operators

These are candidate organizations that match the pilot profile.
Each needs a discovery call to confirm decision-maker access, data availability, and active planning pain.
This list is a starting point, not a validated prospect list.

**Tier 1 — Best fit based on public profile**

- **Indiana Food and Farm Connection** — statewide network supporting local food hubs and farm-to-institution supply. Likely has relationships with institutional buyers and a multi-grower supply base. Entry point into hub operator network.
- **GrowLocal Indiana** — Indianapolis-based food hub focused on regional produce for institutional and wholesale buyers. Recurring demand from institutional buyers makes it a strong planning pain candidate.
- **Limitless Fresh** — Indianapolis-area food hub and distribution operation with restaurant and institutional buyer relationships. Operates recurring local sourcing programs.

**Tier 2 — Worth evaluating**

- **Heartland Harvest** — Midwest-focused local food aggregation. Evaluate whether they serve recurring institutional buyers.
- **Locally Grown Gardens** — multi-site Indiana operation with farm-to-institution experience. Confirm whether planning is managed centrally.
- **The Farm at Prophetstown** — Purdue-adjacent farm operation with institutional connections. Smaller scale but potential Purdue introduction pathway.

**Purdue Entry Points**

- Purdue Extension Agriculture — has direct relationships with Indiana food hub operators and regional produce networks
- Purdue Center for Regional Development — has previously supported Indiana food systems and local food supply chain research
- Purdue Food Science and Agricultural Economics departments — potential credibility validators and introduction sources

These are not endorsements or partnerships.
They are potential introduction pathways that reduce cold outreach friction.

3. request a sample of historical order data before committing to a full pilot
4. build the first manual demand and supply risk view using that data
5. convert one operator into a paid pilot with a clear success metric agreed upfront
